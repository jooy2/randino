# Fictional names and places

Notes on how the fictional generators behave and why, for whoever changes them. They describe the npm package, which is the source of truth, and hold for the Dart and Python ports too: a behaviour that lands in one lands in all three. The repository's own `CLAUDE.md` covers everything that is not one generator's.

`randFictionalName` writes names for the characters of a story or a game; `randFictionalCountry`, `randFictionalRegion`, `randFictionalCity` and `randFictionalDistrict` write the places on its map. They are not `randName` at `realism: 'invented'`, which reads like one real language, and not `randNickname`, which is built from everyday words: a fictional name is English-sounding first and written by its sound everywhere else.

## Behaviour worth knowing before changing it

- **One spelling per script, not per language.** Every entry of every fictional dataset is spelled five times — Latin, Hangul, katakana, hanzi, Cyrillic — and `SCRIPT_OF` in `spelling.ts` says which one a language reads. English, Vietnamese, Spanish, Italian and German keep a made-up name the way it is spelled, so they share the Latin column. A name is one name whatever it is written in: `unique` compares the Latin spelling, and the detail's `forms` reads every column the call asked for.
- **A spelling is the one a reader expects, not a letter-by-letter one.** The pools hold `Alice` as `앨리스` and `Элис`, the way Korean and Russian write that name; a Chinese woman's name takes the characters Chinese gives one (`爱丽丝`, `莉`, `娜`). Only the invented words are spelled by rule, out of parts that each carry their own spelling.
- **An invented word is parts joined in every script at once.** `assemble` in `pools.ts` draws one part per slot — a `head`, at most one `middle`, an ending; or a `front` and a `back` — and `joinParts` joins the five columns. Every part after the first starts on a consonant, so no script merges two vowels. Two things cannot be joined part by part and `joinable` keeps them apart: a doubled letter (`Kallis` is `カリス`, not `カルリス`) and an `sh` before an `l`; it also refuses an open syllable followed by one starting the same way (`Lalan`). Korean is the one script whose join is not concatenation: an `l` after a syllable with no final consonant doubles its `ㄹ`, so `에드` + `린` is `에들린` and `아` + `리아` is `알리아`, and the rule reads the Latin spelling because an `r` does not double (`이스라`).
- **The avoid lists are kept whole by the suite, not by hand.** `test/fictional.test.ts` walks every word the shapes can make — some hundred thousand — and asserts that none is a real country in any of the nine languages, a famous fictional name, or holds a word on its list of obscenities in any script. A collision goes into `FICTIONAL_AVOID` (the Latin spelling of the whole word) or `FICTIONAL_AVOID_INSIDE` (a run of letters); the suite also fails on an entry no shape can make, so the lists stay as short as the data allows. A part added to any table has to pass that walk, and the walk is why the obscenity list lives in the test rather than in the package.
- **No name from one well-known work.** `Arwen`, `Winterfell`, `Hyrule` and their like are out of the pools and on the suite's list, because the name belongs to the work; Arthurian, Greek and Norse names are in, because those stories belong to everybody. A curated name that a script turns into another word is out too: `Kael` is a frog in katakana.
- **`realism` decides per part.** `real` draws from the pools, `invented` from the shapes, and `mixed` decides for each part — a given name and its surname apart. A first character no pool entry starts with is answered with an invented part that does, and a hiragana `startsWith` is read as the katakana every Japanese spelling is written in.
- **The kind of a place is a template per language.** `FICTIONAL_KINDS` writes each kind in all nine languages with `{}` for the name, two templates where a language writes a foreign place both ways: by the English word in its script (`그루거 파크`, `Гругер-парк`) or by its own (`그루거 공원`, `Парк Гругер`). Left to itself, a language translates a country's or a region's kind and borrows a city's or a district's, the way it writes `Death Valley` and `Central Park`; `kindStyle` forces either. A kind that no borrowed word exists for in a language holds its own word in both columns, and Chinese never borrows.
- **A kind the name already says is drawn again.** `Port Galeport` and `Highmoor Moors` are what a kind drawn apart from its name writes, so `draft` refuses a kind whose code, less a final `s`, is in the name.
- **`startsWith` reads the name, the length options read the place.** A kind written in front (`Kingdom of Edlin`, `Королевство Эдлин`) is not where the place's name starts. The length range is met by planning: a gender, a kind and a surname are each chosen so the rest can still land inside it, and each invented part is drawn from the entries that leave the parts after it room — the same reasoning the organization generator follows. A range the pools cannot meet is answered with the closest.
- **The odds are data.** `FICTIONAL_KIND_CHANCE`, `FICTIONAL_MIDDLE_CHANCE` and `FICTIONAL_SYLLABLE_CHANCE` live in `data/index.ts` and are compared by `tools/parity`.

## The datasets

The tables are written once, here, and `node tools/emit/index.mjs` writes the Dart and Python copies from them. Edit a table in `data/` and regenerate; the ports' copies say at the top that they are generated. `data/index.ts`, holding the chances, is written by hand in all three packages.

Adding to a table:

1. Spell the entry in all five scripts the way a reader of each expects it, and check it against the rules above: a well-known work's name, a real country, a word a script turns it into.
2. For a syllable or a half, run the JavaScript suite: the walk reports every word the new part makes that it must not, by its Latin spelling.
3. Run `node tools/emit/index.mjs`, every suite, and `node tools/parity/index.mjs`.
